# Changelog

Newest first. Each entry is dated by when it landed.

## 2026-11-12: Latheport 1.5.0 (was ConvertAll), a photo editor, a video timeline, PDF tools, smart tools, no Ghostscript needed

In numbers, against ConvertAll 1.4.0 (both built, the same files on the same PC, taking turns, best of 3): 25 PostScript files to PDF in 2.77 s instead of 4.94 s (1.8x), with no Ghostscript to install (24 MB less); 20 DjVu books to PDF in 61.7 s instead of 76.3 s; 12 RAW photos to PNG in 60.0 s instead of 65.5 s. The download is 78.3 MB, against 77.0 MB for 1.4.0, with the editor, the timeline and the smart tools in it (the smart tools' models download on first use).

- **Fixed: text that sat a little off in the headers**, found by measuring the pixels of every screen in both themes. The version beside "Latheport" is now centred on the name (it sat 4 px off), the editor's "Edit" is centred over its tool column (it was 11 px left), and the editor's Text bar no longer has its size slider under the Colour button when several files are open (the editor now also won't shrink narrower than its bar needs).
- **Fixed: a password-protected PDF said it was "protected by DRM"** when you chose pages, split or compress. It now says it is password-protected and can't be opened, as everywhere else. PDFs that only have permissions set (open with no password, no editing allowed) are edited, joined, split and compressed, and keep the same permissions (tested).
- **Fixed: an RPM, installer or .pkg packed more than 200 to 1 was refused as damaged** (90 MB of zeros in a 90 KB RPM). The limit is now the free space on the drive, as for every other archive.
- **Fixed: playing a 3D animation stopped with "Singular matrix"** when a part was scaled down to 0, the usual way to hide one.
- **Fixed: a picture of more than 268 million pixels gave a message about "pixport" and `max_pixels=`.** It now says the picture is larger than Latheport opens.
- **ConvertAll is now Latheport.** It does more than convert now (an editor, a video timeline, PDF tools, smart tools), and "ConvertAll" was a crowded name. Latheport joins its engines, bookport, pixport, meshport and packport. The program is `Latheport.exe` (and `latheport-cli.exe` in a terminal), with a new icon, a lathe's chuck turning, and the window in its copper instead of indigo (easier to read, too: white on a button went from a contrast of 4.47 to 5.18, past the 4.5 that WCAG asks for). Nothing to set up again: the first time it opens, your settings, presets, watch folders, last formats and update choices come along, downloaded engines and smart tools move over (renamed in place, not downloaded again), and the right-click entries and Start menu shortcut point at Latheport instead. ConvertAll's own settings are left as they were. Entries below 1.5 keep the old name.
- **Added: Update now.** When a newer Latheport is out, the banner's **Update now** (with its size) downloads it with progress and *Cancel*, checks it against the SHA-256 GitHub lists, and unpacks it next to your copy; **Restart to update** opens the new version in the same folder, so the right-click menu and Start menu shortcut still point at it. The old copy is kept until the new one has opened, and is put back by itself if it doesn't: checked end to end with the real launcher, both ways. It always jumps to the newest version, however many were skipped. Nothing is downloaded without the click. ConvertAll 1.4's link-only check still finds the release under the new name: tested against a stand-in for GitHub's rename redirect.
- **Fixed: `from latheport import plan` failed** (it did in 1.4 too, as `from convertall import plan`). It now gives a conversion's plan without converting, exactly what `--plan --json` prints.
- **Added: deep fry.** The meme look for any picture: colours, contrast and sharpness turned way up, grain, and the crunch of a JPG saved over and over. *Deep fry* in the editor is a slider from 1% (a light crisp) to 100% (fried to blocks, the words still readable), or `--deepfry 1` to `100`. Every step up is further from the original (on a test picture, 25 at 1% to 85 at 100%, average difference per pixel out of 255). The picture keeps its size and transparency, animated GIFs are fried frame by frame, and the same picture always fries the same way. It takes 0.15 to 0.3 s for a 1600 x 1200 JPG and about 1.5 s for a 24-megapixel photo. Like a cut, it's for the files at hand: it isn't remembered for the next ones.
- **Added: picture effects you'd use every day**, in the editor next to *Deep fry*, or from the command line. Like a cut, they're for the files at hand: they aren't remembered for the next ones.
  - *Turn* a picture a quarter right or left, upside down, or mirrored (`--rotate right`, `left`, `180`, `mirror`).
  - *Crop to* a square, 4:5 for posts, 16:9 wide or 9:16 for stories and reels, from the middle (`--crop 4:5`). The check after converting confirms the new width and height.
  - *Touch up*, *Auto fix colour and contrast* (`--enhance auto`): on a washed-out, blue-tinted test photo, the average difference from the original went from 23.6 to 6.5 (out of 255); a good photo changes by 0.3.
  - *Touch up*, *Clean up a page* (`--enhance page`) turns a phone photo of a page into white paper and dark writing, in grey. On a test page with a shadow across it, the paper went from 185 ± 32 to an even 255 and the writing from 53 to 27. Use it with PDF for a clean scan in one step.
  - *Colour*: black and white (a real one-channel grey file, smaller) or sepia (`--colour bw`, `sepia`).
  - All of them keep transparency and treat every frame of a GIF alike. For a 1600 x 1200 picture each takes under 0.1 s. For a 24-megapixel photo, turning, cropping and colour cost at most 0.1 s more than a plain save (0.15 s), and the touch-ups about 0.9 s in all.
- **Added: the editor.** *Edit…* in Options (or the pencil on a dropped picture or video) opens a dark photo-editor window: *Crop*, *Adjust* and *Text* down the left (keys C, A, T), the tool's options along the top, the files in a strip along the bottom. Undo and Redo, Compare (hold it, or `\`), zoom where the pointer is, Space-drag to look around.
  - *Crop*: drag a box or its corners, or lock it to *Original*, *1:1*, *4:5*, *16:9* or *9:16*; turn and mirror with the box turning along. Guides show as in photo apps: a grid of thirds while the box moves, a line when it's in the middle, edges that snap to the picture's, and a label with the box's size in pixels that names the shape when it is one (*1080 × 1350 · 4:5*). The result is exactly the box chosen, checked after converting. From the command line: `--area 10,5,80,60` (left, top, width, height in percent).
  - Edits go to all the files, or to one: *All files*, *This file* or *As it is* for the file shown. A crop locked to a shape fits each file's own size (a 4:5 crop of a landscape photo and of a portrait one are both 4:5).
  - *Turn* can now also mirror and turn together (`--rotate mirror-right`, `mirror-left`, `flip`), so any way a picture lies can be put right.
- **Changed: fewer settings in Options.** Turn, Crop to, Touch up, Colour, Deep fry and the watermark moved into the editor; for a photo and a video together Options went from 17 settings to 11. The command line keeps every flag.
- **Fixed: Options no longer run off the bottom of the screen.** Opening Options for a video grew the window to fit every setting, past the screen's edge where the last ones couldn't be reached. Now they open in a panel beside the column, under headings (*Picture*, *Video*, *Sound*, *Cut and size*, *Speed*), and the window gets wider, never taller: on a 1080p screen it goes from 460 x 500 to 856 x 500, and back when Options close. What doesn't fit scrolls inside the panel. Switching formats no longer leaves the last format's rows showing over the new ones for a moment.
- **Fixed: plain words for a damaged file.** Instead of a library's message with the file's full temporary path ("cannot identify image file 'C:\\Users\\...'"), it says "broken.png doesn't open as a PNG picture: it may be damaged, or not really a PNG". No message shows a full path any more.
- **Changed: a newer version shows in a slim banner** under the header, with *See what's new* and *Not now* (that version isn't brought up again), instead of a link squeezed beside the name.
- **Fixed: closing Latheport while something was still working in the background could end in a crash report** (a download, an engine install, a check for updates or a 3D model loading): it now closes quietly every time (0 crashes in 12 tries, against 4 to 6 in 10 before).
- **Fixed: `latheport --help` stopped with an error** once a setting's description held a percent sign; it prints in full again.
- **Fixed: little things a look through every screen found.** Tick boxes showed no box in the light theme; the values in the boxes you can type into sat 5 pixels right of the others; About closed with a different button from the other windows; *Watch folders* with no folders was a blank box (it says what to do now); the editor offered tools for a file it couldn't show.
- **Fixed: what a review of the window found** (each with a test that failed before the fix):
  - Changing a setting with a format other than the suggested one chosen rebuilt every Options row twice, so the field you were typing in lost the keyboard and a slider being dragged was dropped. The rows now stay.
  - After closing to the notification area once, closing the window never quit Latheport again, even with *Keep watching from the notification area* turned off. It quits again.
  - The check for updates ran once, 4 s after Latheport opened, and counted as done before it asked: offline at start-up meant no check that day, and a window left open for days never checked again. It now tries again an hour later when GitHub can't be reached, and checks each day while the window stays open.
  - *Show in folder* picked the wrong file when its name had a comma in it (`a,b.png`).
  - The dropped picture was made at 240 x 120 pixels and blown up on a 150% or 200% screen; it's made at the screen's own pixels now (480 x 240 at 200%), sharp.
  - A watched folder's file that stopped or failed was described with the files in the window ("1 saved, 2 not converted" for one file); it's told by its own name now. A watched folder that isn't there (renamed, a drive unplugged) shows as *not there* in *Watch folders*, and from the notification area a file that fails says so in a notification.
  - The pencil on the dropped file still opened the editor during a conversion (for a PDF the window could freeze until the conversion was done), and each click opened another editor. It waits now, and a second click brings the open one to the front.
  - A 3D model whose chosen take doesn't move in time (all its keys at one moment) stopped playing with an error on every frame; a minimised window kept playing the drop area's 3D preview unseen. Both fixed.
  - An XPS page that wasn't readable came out as a raw "ParseError" for any page but the first (it says which page is damaged now), and each page was read twice (the first three times).
  - *Details* printed the editor's settings as stored (a crop as "10,5,80,60", texts and drawings as code); it says them in words now ("cropped, 1 text, black and white").
- **Fixed: what a review of the conversions found** (each with a test that failed before the fix):
  - *Update now* failed whenever Latheport had been opened from its Start menu shortcut or by a double-click: those run it in its own folder, and Windows won't rename a folder a running program is in, so the swap gave up after 30 s and said the update had failed. The update helper now leaves that folder first, and the new version runs in its own. What earlier updates left (the old copy and a version that didn't start, about 230 MB each, and the helper's folder in %TEMP%) is now removed, also beside an app folder with [ ] in its name. Only folders an update moved aside are removed: a folder of your own named `Latheport.old` or `Latheport.failed` (a backup, say) is never deleted, and if one is in the way the update stops and says so instead.
  - A YAML file of 281 bytes whose aliases nest six deep (each naming the one before ten times) became 13 MB of JSON, and three more levels would have been 13 GB, written where Cancel can't stop it. It's refused with how many values it would expand to (12,345,685); ordinary anchors and `<<:` merges work as before.
  - Speech recognition let FFmpeg follow web addresses inside a file (an .mp3 that's really a playlist). It reads local files only, as every other FFmpeg step does.
  - *Upscale* 2x filled a 4x picture of float numbers first: about 50 times the result's size in memory (370 MB for a 0.6-megapixel photo; a 20-megapixel one, inside the limit, would have asked for gigabytes). It now keeps 3 bytes a pixel of the result: 21 MB for the same photo, the same picture (4x is byte for byte the same, 2x within 2 levels on 0.2% of values).
  - An animated GIF, WebP or PNG gave every frame the first frame's time (100, 2000 and 100 ms became 100, 100 and 100). Each frame keeps its own.
  - `notes.txt.gz` to XZ was named `notes.txt.gz.xz` (and extracted to a text file named `notes.txt.gz`); it's `notes.txt.xz`.
  - A CSV, JSON or spreadsheet result between 50 and 200 MB was reported as "doesn't open"; it's just not checked again, as other big files aren't.
  - Pictures and subtitles laid over a video failed when a folder in the way had an apostrophe in its name ("Bob's files"), which the temp folder has when the Windows user name does.
  - *Cancel* now stops inside long steps, not only between them: between the pages of a PDF made into pictures or text and of a comic, between the tiles of an upscale, and a megabyte at a time inside a big file in a TAR archive. Before, a 1,000-page PDF drew every page first.
  - Columns with the same name in a CSV or spreadsheet overwrote each other (`name,value,value` kept only the second value); the second is now "value 2". XML text after a child element (`<p>Hello <b>world</b> again</p>`) was dropped. Converting to TOML or plist now says that empty (null) values are left out.
  - An engine that ran too long ended with its whole command line ("Command '[...soffice.com', '--headless'...]' timed out after 600 seconds"); it says "soffice took longer than 10 minutes, so it was stopped".
  - A text file in UTF-16 without a byte order mark (some Excel and Windows exports) came out full of empty characters; it's read as UTF-16.
  - An email or Outlook message saved as HTML kept the mail's `on...=` scripts, `javascript:` links and pictures from the web (a sender's tracking pixel tells them it was opened); they're taken out, and the mail's own pictures and links stay. Pictures a mail's own style asked for from the web (`background:url(https://...)`, `@import`) and links split to dodge the check (`java<tab>script:`) are taken out too. An MHT web archive whose part was named `a.png` also rewrote the address `a.png.bak`, and the pictures its stylesheets load were left out; both fixed.
  - Two programs finishing a file of the same name into one folder at the same moment (the window and `latheport watch`) could overwrite one result with the other; the second now gets `name (1)`.
  - `--plan` could show a different route than the conversion takes (one needing an engine that isn't installed), and a bad setting given to `plan()` raised a ValueError instead of saying what's wrong.
  - An error message naming two files ("Couldn't rename C:\...\b.txt to C:\...\d.txt") came out as "Couldn't rename d.txt"; each path is cut to its own name.
  - A file left half written when an archive entry turned out damaged, too big or cancelled part-way is now removed.
  - A linear-light picture (an XCF in linear precision, an EXR) made into a JPEG kept its own colour profile though it was given the sRGB curve, so viewers showed it far too bright; the profile is left out for those now.
  - A different page or file count in the check after converting was put down to whatever the conversion noted first, even a note about something else; only a note about that count explains it now.
- **Added: the keyboard can do it all in the window.** Ctrl+O chooses files (or Tab to the drop area and press Enter), Ctrl+Enter converts, Esc cancels. In the editor, Ctrl+V pastes into a text.
- **Fixed: the editor, before its first release.** Dragging the text *Size* slider made an Undo step for every value it passed (28 for one drag, pushing older steps out of the 100 kept); it's one step now, and the slider keeps the keyboard. Left, Right, Page Up and Page Down while typing a text switched to another file; they stay in the text. Closing the editor or the Pages window with work still running waited 3 s for each job in turn (12 s frozen with four), and could end Latheport with a crash report when that work went on after the window closed, or was still going when Latheport quit; it waits 3 s for all of them together now, and lets them finish safely. FFmpeg missing while a video was paused left the rough frame up with no word. The *Done* button turned lavender under the mouse instead of a lighter copper.
- **Faster in the editor:** with a see-through picture zoomed in 8 times, each redraw (every caret blink and every move of a drag) took 1.15 s; it takes 0.015 s (the checkerboard is one fill of what's on the screen, and texts and drawings are made at the picture's own pixels, not 8 times them). Dragging a text at that zoom: 1.9 s a move, now 0.21 s. At the fitted size a redraw takes 5 ms instead of 10, the picture identical. Video frames are copied once on their way to the screen instead of twice (4.0 ms a 1080p frame, now 2.0). A watermark's corner is checked without copying the whole photo first (61 ms on a 24-megapixel photo, now 18).
- **Changed: no title bar.** The window's own header is its title bar now, with the version beside the name; drag it to move the window, double-click to maximise. Windows still snaps it to the screen's edges, shows Windows 11's snap layouts over the maximise button, resizes it from every edge and gives it its shadow. Every other window has its own title bar too: the editor, the 3D viewer, *Engines*, *Watch folders*, *Conversion details*, *About*, the colour picker, and every question and message, which now answer in plain words (*Stop and close*, *Keep converting*). Small windows can't be maximised or stretched by their edges; the viewer, *Watch folders* and *Details* can.
- **Added: text on pictures and videos.** In the editor, *Text* works as in Photoshop: click anywhere on the picture and type there, with the cursor where the next letter goes. The bar at the top sets the font (Sans, Serif, Bold or Handwriting), size, colour and an outline or a shadow, for the next text or for the one clicked. Click a text to type in it or drag it; Enter or Esc when done; any number of texts. *Meme* adds big upper-case captions at the top and bottom, white with a black outline; long ones wrap onto up to three lines and stay inside the picture. From the command line: `--text "..."`, again for more, with `--text-at top|middle|bottom` and `--text-style meme`. The four fonts come with Latheport (976 KB, all under the SIL Open Font License), so text looks the same on every PC.
- **Added: a watermark**, your own text small in a corner, see-through, dark where the corner is bright so it always shows (`--watermark "my-site.example"`, `--watermark-at`). There is none unless you type one, and once typed it's remembered.
- **Added: video effects.** *Turn*, *Crop to* (or by hand), *Colour* (black and white, sepia), text and the watermark work on videos, GIFs and animated WebP too. A phone video saved upright with a rotation tag is cropped as you see it. On a 20 s 1080p clip, a plain conversion takes 3.5 s; turned 3.8 s, cropped square 2.5 s, black and white 3.3 s, two texts and a watermark 3.9 s.
- **Added: speed and backwards for sound and video.** *Speed* from 0.25x to 4x: voices keep their pitch (a 440 Hz test tone stays at 440 Hz at 2x), or set *Pitch* to change with it, like a tape (880 Hz at 2x). The length is checked afterwards (8 s at 2x: 4.0 s). *Backwards* plays it in reverse frame for frame, sound included, a few seconds at a time so it needs little memory: a 40 s 1080p video in 10.4 s, exactly its 1,200 frames. It takes up to 10 minutes. Speed and Backwards are for the files at hand; Pitch is remembered.
- Text and watermarks add little time: on a 24-megapixel photo, 0.31 s for two meme captions and 0.36 s for a watermark, against 0.15 s for a plain save (0.04 s each on a 1600 x 1200 picture).
- **Added: Adjust, in the editor.** Sliders for brightness, contrast, saturation, warmth and sharpness (-100 to 100), shown on the picture as you move them, or `--adjust brightness=15,warmth=20`. Each moves the picture its way: on a test photo, brightness +50 took its average from 151 to 173 (and -50 to 124), contrast +50 its spread from 47 to 63, saturation -100 its colour to none. Videos get the same through FFmpeg, within 1.5 to 3.5 levels (out of 255) of what the editor shows. A 24-megapixel photo takes 0.65 s with all five, against 0.13 s for a plain save; a 20 s 1080p video 4.1 s against 2.6 s.
- **Added: Straighten.** Turn a crooked horizon level, up to 15 degrees either way in tenths, with a grid over the picture while you drag; it's cut in just enough that no corner is empty, and keeps its size (`--straighten -2.5`). Pictures and videos alike: 1.1 s for a 24-megapixel photo (0.13 s plain), 4.0 s for a 20 s 1080p video (2.6 s plain).
- **Added: Hide.** In the editor, drag a box over a face, a number plate or a password and choose *Pixelate*, *Blur* or *Cover* (solid black, the safe way to hide text), with a strength slider; or `--hide pixelate:40,55,12,8` (again for more). Not a pixel outside the box changes (tested on pictures pixel for pixel). It works on every kind of picture and on every frame of a video, and costs almost nothing: 0.17 s for three boxes on a 24-megapixel photo (0.13 s plain), 2.7 s on a 20 s 1080p video (2.6 s plain).
- **Added: Draw.** Arrows, lines, boxes and ellipses (Shift for 45 degrees and squares), the pen, a see-through highlighter, and stickers: a tick, a cross, a star, a heart and numbers that count up as you put them down. Colour, width and fill are in the panel; *Select* picks a drawing up to move or delete it. Drawings are placed on the picture itself, so they stay on what they point at when the crop changes, and they turn with it. Ten drawings take 0.39 s on a 24-megapixel photo and 2.9 s on a 20 s 1080p video (2.6 s plain).
- **Added: a picture as the watermark, and an opacity for it.** Your logo (a PNG with a see-through background works best), your text or both, small in a corner, as solid as the *Opacity* slider says (5 to 100%: at 40% it's drawn at exactly 40%). Kept for next time like the text one (`--watermark-image logo.png --watermark-opacity 40`). If the picture is moved or deleted, the conversion says so in plain words instead of leaving it out unnoticed.
- **Added: videos play in the editor, with a timeline to trim them.** Space plays and pauses with the sound, comma and period step one frame (exactly: 1/25 s on a 25 fps video), and a strip of pictures under the stage shows the whole video; click or drag on it to go anywhere. Drag either end of the strip to keep just a part, or press I and O where you are. The new *Trim* tool (V) sets the speed (0.25x to 4x, pitch kept or changing) and backwards, and saves the frame shown as a picture next to the video with every edit on it, in 0.14 s for a 720p frame. Trims and speed are each video's own, so trimming one clip doesn't trim the others. Playback is [puretech-player](https://github.com/puretechteam/puretech-player): the first frame shows 71 ms after you press play, a seek lands on the exact frame, and the picture strip for a 6 s 720p video is ready in 0.17 s.
- **Added: a paused video shows exactly what the conversion makes.** While a video plays the editor shows it turned, cropped, drawn on and in black and white; paused or moved, FFmpeg makes the frame with every effect through the conversion's own filters (80 ms for a 720p frame), so what you see is what you get. Compared with FFmpeg's own frame at that moment, the editor's picture differs by under 2 levels out of 255.
- **Added: PDF tools.** A PDF can be converted to a PDF to change its pages:
  - *Edit…* opens the **Pages** window: every page as a picture, to drag into another order, turn or take out, with Undo; the pages picked can be saved as a PDF of their own. From the command line: `--pages 3,1-2,5r`.
  - **Join** PDFs (tick *Join*): their pages one after another in one PDF, with every bookmark; in the Pages window they show as one grid, to merge and order at once.
  - **Split into** PDFs of any number of pages, together in a folder (`--split 1` for one PDF per page), each named for its pages and carrying the bookmarks of its own pages.
  - **Compress**: *Smaller* (pictures at 150 dpi) or *Balanced* (200 dpi), text staying text. A 4-page 300 dpi scan of 3.11 MB became 0.67 MB (Smaller) or 1.27 MB (Balanced), and drawn at 150 dpi the worst page kept a PSNR of 34.5 and 35.7 dB.
  - Bookmarks follow their pages (those for pages taken out go) and the title, links and form fields stay: on an 85-page book, keeping 49 pages in a new order kept its three remaining chapters pointing at the right pages. The check after converting counts the pages (85 across 5 split parts: the same). Protected PDFs are left as they are, said in words. All of it is bookport's, which reads and writes PDFs itself; PDFium only draws the pages in the window.
- **Added: smart tools**, each run on this computer (nothing is sent anywhere) and downloaded once the first time it's needed, asked first with its size: the window offers it as it does FFmpeg, the command line asks or takes `-y`. Each download is pinned and checked against its SHA-256, and it's kept whole or not at all. ONNX Runtime comes with the first one (15 MB). They keep to three quarters of the cores, so the computer stays usable.
  - **Background** (190 MB): *Taken out* (see-through), *White*, *Black* or *Blurred* like a phone's portrait mode, in the editor's *Adjust* panel with the result shown, or `--background remove`. BiRefNet finds the subject. On 15 test pictures with things in the background touching the subject, it found it with an IoU of 0.988 or more (0.994 on average); IS-Net, the other model tried, took in part of the background on 7 of them (IoU 0.75 to 0.87). The edges are then fitted to the picture's own (28% less error at the edges on 12 test pictures), and their colour freed of the old background, so no fringe shows on a new one. On a 16-thread PC a 2-megapixel photo takes 5.8 s and a 12-megapixel one 8.5 s, the model's loading included, with at most 2.0 GB of memory in use.
  - **Upscale** 2x or 4x (5 MB): Real-ESRGAN draws in sharp edges where resizing blurs (`--upscale 2`). On text made 4x smaller and back, it came out 0.8 dB closer to the original than bicubic resizing at 4x and 4.6 dB at 2x. On soft, blurred photos it sharpens what was soft, so plain resizing measures closer there. It works in tiles, so memory stays flat for any size, and no seams show where they meet. A 640 x 480 picture takes 3.7 s at 2x and 10.4 s at 4x (most of that saving the 4.9-megapixel PNG), with 290 MB of memory.
  - **Picture to text** (OCR, 10 MB): any picture, or every page of a TIFF scan, becomes a text file. PaddleOCR's models read Latin letters (English, French, German, Spanish and 28 more). On 10 test pages in two fonts, sizes from 14 to 44 pixels, blurred and tilted 2 degrees, 1.2% of characters came out wrong; a tilted page is read levelled. *Copy the text in it* in the editor puts a picture's text on the clipboard. An A4 page at 300 dpi takes 1.8 s; a page of 11-point text came out without a single mistake.
  - **Make searchable**, for PDFs (`--searchable yes`): scanned pages are read and their words laid over them unseen, each on its own word, so the PDF can be searched and its text copied, also on pages stored turned. On a test scan, a word found by PDFium lay within 3 points of where the picture has it. Pages with text of their own stay as they are. Five scanned A4 pages take 16 s, 3.2 s a page.
  - **Speech to subtitles and text** (Whisper, 80 MB): sound and video become SRT, VTT, ASS (and the other subtitle formats) or a transcript (`latheport talk.mp3 --to srt`). From a video, choose *Subtitles from: What's said* (`--subtitles-from speech`); or add them to a video, *Burned into the picture* (in the Inter font, sized for the picture) or *As a subtitle track* in MP4, MKV, MOV or WebM (`--speech-subs burn`). Cut and speed are followed. The language is heard in the first 30 seconds or set (`--language fr`, 99 languages). Subtitles are at most two lines of 42 characters, and silence is skipped (Whisper writes "you" over it otherwise). On speech made with Windows' own voice, every word came out right but for numbers written as figures ("9" for "nine"). 33 s of speech takes 2.6 s and 3 min 16 s takes 18 s, about 11 times as fast as it plays, with 540 MB of memory.

- **Fixed (security): extracting archives is safer.** An audit of every way ConvertAll unpacks archives (ZIP, 7z, TAR in all its compressions, CPIO, single-file compressors, and 7-Zip for RAR, ISO, RPM and the rest) found gaps, now closed. Extracting a comic or repacking an archive goes through the same checks. New rules, the same for every extractor (`convertall/safety.py`):
  - **Nothing lands outside the folder.** Names with `..` are left out. Leading slashes and drive letters are dropped, as unzip and tar do.
  - **No links.** Symbolic links, hard links that point out, junctions and device files are left out. A sweep after every extraction removes any link that slipped through, without following it. A hard link inside a TAR becomes a copy of its file.
  - **Names Windows can't take are renamed the way 7-Zip does:** `a:b` (which would have written a hidden alternate data stream) becomes `a_b`, `CON.txt` becomes `_CON.txt`, and a trailing dot or space becomes `_`. Before, these failed or wrote somewhere unexpected.

Links are excluded either way. Password prompts can no longer hang a conversion.

- **Added:** FBX textures are found even when the file points at the original artist's PC. ConvertAll looks next to the model and in `Textures` folders up to two levels above it. In testing this raised the share of textures found from 24% to 74%; the rest don't exist on disk.
- **Faster:** FBX → OBJ is written directly. STL skips merging textures it can't store, which took seconds on scenes with hundreds of parts.
- **Changed:** rigged or animated FBX files convert as static models in their rest pose. Blender carried the skeleton and animation into GLB. FBX files with no model in them (animation clips) now give a clear error.

## 2026-10-25: ConvertAll 1.0, first public release

- **Converts** images, audio, video, subtitles, documents, spreadsheets, presentations, drawings, PDFs, comics, ebooks, 3D models, CAD, DICOM, data files and fonts to each other. It also compresses or extracts archives. It chains steps when there's no direct route (docx → pdf → png). See the formats table in the README.
- **Added:** when you drop a file in, the most common conversion for it is already picked (JPG → PNG, MP4 → MP3, DOCX → PDF, ...). After that, ConvertAll remembers your choice per file type. `convertall FILE --list` shows the most common choice too.
- **Fixed:** comics wouldn't convert to PDF.
  - CBZ, CBR, CB7 and CBT → PDF works, with pages in reading order and JPEG/PNG pages kept as they are, not re-compressed.
  - A comic with the wrong extension (a "cbr" that's really a zip) is detected by its content.
  - PDF → CBZ extracts page images without loss.
  - A folder of images → PDF or CBZ.
- **Added formats:**
  - **Subtitles:** SRT, VTT, ASS, SSA, and pulling the subtitle track out of a video.
  - **Drawings:** Visio, Publisher, CorelDRAW, ODG.
  - **Office:** macro-enabled and template files.
  - **Images:** EMF/WMF, AI, SGI, XBM, CUR, XPM, and PDF → SVG.
  - **Audio:** M4B, WavPack, CAF, MP2, AU, APE.
  - **Video:** VOB, ASF, F4V, MXF, RealMedia.
  - **Data:** JSONL, plist, SQLite.
  - **Archives:** tar.zst and zst.
  - **Video → animated WebP.**
- **Fixed**, found by converting every format to every format it can reach (4,262 conversions):
  - **ICO:** images smaller than 16 px.
  - **INI:** tables now become numbered sections. Key case is kept, and files without a section header can be read.
  - **WavPack:** MP3 input.
  - **MP2:** low-sample-rate input.
  - **GIF:** no longer offers nonsense routes like GIF → video → audio.
  - **CSV → XLSX:** numbers become real numbers, and leading zeros (zip codes, IDs) are kept as text.
  - **Temporary folders:** cleaned up even when Windows still has a file open.
